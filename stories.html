Perovskite Nanomaterials / 2025

Two ligands. Fewer surface losses.

Blue-emitting nanoplatelets expose a large fraction of their atoms at the surface. DDAB supplies bromide for vacancy passivation, while HPA coordinates undercoordinated lead; together they suppress pathways that dissipate excitation without light.

Why it matters Complementary surface chemistry can make blue perovskite emission both brighter and more stable.

Perovskite Nanomaterials / 2024

Bromide-rich surfaces brighten blue emission.

Thin perovskite nanoplatelets are sensitive to surface halide defects. Introducing ZnBr2 during synthesis increases bromide availability and changes the balance between carrier trapping and light emission.

Why it matters Precursor chemistry offers a route to control the surface while the nanocrystal forms.

A thinner screen reveals finer X-ray detail.

Seeing the hidden structure of everyday objects.

An additive changes more than the brightness.

Adding ZnBr2 to CsPbBr3 nanocrystals changes surface chemistry and can also redirect phase formation toward Cs4PbBr6-containing structures. Composition, phase, and optical response therefore have to be evaluated together.
