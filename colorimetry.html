Your 1° viewing field

Chromaticity calculations use the CIE 1931 2° standard observer, which ISO/CIE specifies for fields of about 1°–4°. The official color-matching functions are tabulated every 1 nm from 360 to 830 nm. Rendering metrics and biological weighting use the observers and action spectra prescribed by their respective methods, as documented below.

Principle and interpretation

CRI/Ra and R1–R15 use the CIE 2024 sample set in Colour 0.4.7; Ra remains the average of the first eight samples. CRI is withheld when the source–reference distance in CIE 1960 uv exceeds 0.0054. CQS reports NIST CQS 9.0 Qa, Qf and Qg. TM-30 reports the ANSI/IES TM-30-18 implementation, including all 99 sample scores in the CSV export. The method edition is recorded with each result.

This workspace reports rendition for near-white sources at 2,000–25,000 K and |Duv| ≤ 0.05. CRI and CQS use the 2° observer; TM-30 uses its prescribed 10° sample colorimetry and 2° CCT calculation. The chromaticity diagrams retain your CIE 1931 observer setting. Numerical values may vary slightly with sampling and method edition; use the current official IES tool for a standards report.

07 / LED & WHITE-LIGHT EFFICACY

Connect optical output to electrical input.

Enter measured device values. The selected spectrum supplies luminous efficacy of radiation and the photon-energy weighting used for EQE.

Use values from the same operating point. Total radiant flux requires a calibrated total-output measurement, such as an integrating sphere. A spectrum used with that flux must represent the same output and include all relevant emission. Current is needed for EQE even when electrical power is entered directly.

Add the measurements available; each result shows which inputs it needs.

Principle and outcome

Electrical power = V × I. Device luminous efficacy = luminous flux / electrical power (lm/W electrical). Wall-plug efficiency = radiant flux / electrical power. Spectral LER = 683 ∫S(λ)V(λ)dλ / ∫S(λ)dλ (lm/W optical), using all supplied radiant power in the denominator and the 360–830 nm photopic function in the numerator. Missing ultraviolet or infrared emission biases a whole-source LER.

EQE = emitted photons / injected electrons. For radiant-power spectra, photon rate = radiant flux × the power-weighted mean wavelength / (hc); electron rate = I/e. Without a spectrum, the entered wavelength supplies a narrow-band approximation. IQE = EQE / extraction efficiency only when extraction has been independently determined. Photoluminescence quantum yield is a separate measurement. Current efficiency = measured luminous intensity / I (cd/A); no angular-distribution assumption is applied.

When measured lumens are absent, luminous flux can be estimated from matched radiant flux × spectral LER. Such output is explicitly labeled as spectrum-derived.

Principle, required ranges and scope

α-opic irradiance = ∫Eλsα(λ)dλ. ELR is weighted irradiance divided by photopic illuminance; DER divides that ratio by the corresponding D65 value. EDI = illuminance × DER. With source luminance, the analogous results are α-opic radiance and equivalent daylight luminance (EDL). Alternatively, scale the spectrum to a measured irradiance or radiance integrated over the exact uploaded wavelength interval; this supports UV/IR-only sources without a photopic normalization. ELR and DER are undefined when photopic output is zero. The five action spectra are the official CIE S 026 data. Calculations use 380–780 nm, including V(λ) normalization over that interval.

The blue-light factor uses B(λ) over 300–700 nm from ICNIRP 2013, Table 2. Multiplying the factor by illuminance gives EB (W·m⁻²); multiplying by luminance gives LB (W·m⁻²·sr⁻¹). Steady dose = weighted value × duration. Retinal thermal radiance uses the revised R(λ) over 380–1400 nm and is reported only for luminance input. UV-A (315–400 nm) and infrared (780–3000 nm) outputs are unweighted band quantities.

Coverage is checked for each quantity. Missing wavelengths are treated as zero only when you explicitly select the zero-extension assumption. A visible-only file cannot establish absence of UV/IR. These results characterize spectra and steady exposures; they do not assign an IEC 62471 risk group, a safe viewing time, or predict a health outcome. A safety assessment also needs calibrated spectroradiometry, source geometry, averaging field and the applicable exposure conditions.

METHOD / SCOPE

Principle and interpretation.

Coordinates and spectra

Emission XYZ is obtained by integrating spectral radiant power with the official CIE x̄, ȳ, z̄ functions. Linear interpolation is used between supplied samples; products of the interpolated functions are integrated across their combined breakpoints. XYZ is normalized to Y = 100.

x = X/(X+Y+Z), y = Y/(X+Y+Z); u′ = 4x/(−2x+12y+3), v′ = 9y/(−2x+12y+3). For CIE 1960, u = u′ and v = 2v′/3.

CCT and Duv

A numerical minimum search finds the closest Planckian radiator in CIE 1960 uv over 1,000–100,000 K. Duv is the signed distance: positive above, negative below the locus. CCT is withheld for |Duv| > 0.05 or when the nearest result reaches the calculation limits.

A saturated red, green or blue emitter can have valid color coordinates while a white-light CCT is not applicable.

Wavelength and excitation purity

Extend a line from the chosen white through the sample to the spectral boundary. Excitation purity is the sample-to-white distance divided by boundary-to-white distance in xy. For purple-side colors, the complementary wavelength is reported and purity uses the purple boundary.

At the reference white, purity is zero and dominant wavelength is undefined. These quantities depend on the chosen reference white.

What needs more information?

RGB gamut needs three primary colors. Peak wavelength, FWHM, spectral centroid and visible-band luminous efficacy need spectral data. Absolute luminance/flux needs calibrated radiometric data and measurement geometry.

The added color-rendition and photobiology sections calculate these quantities from spectral data. LED device efficiency additionally requires electrical and calibrated optical measurements. Coordinates alone supply chromaticity, CCT/Duv and white-point relationships.
